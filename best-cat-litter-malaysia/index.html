Types Compared

Every type of cat litter, explained

Understanding what each type offers helps you make the right choice for your cat and your home.

Bentonite (Clumping Clay)

Most popular in Malaysia. Clumps on contact with liquid for easy scooping. Activated carbon variants like Sanking neutralize odor at molecular level. Fine grain varieties are gentle on paws. 99% dust-free formulas available. Excellent value per day of use.

Best clumping Best odor control Higher weight

Tofu Cat Litter

Made from soy pulp. Lightweight, flushable, and biodegradable. Popular with eco-conscious owners. However, can be more expensive per use, and in Malaysia's humid climate can develop mold faster. Weaker clumping than bentonite.

Eco-friendly Flushable Higher cost

Crystal / Silica Gel

Silica gel beads absorb moisture and lock odors. Long-lasting — one bag can last weeks. However, non-clumping means solids must be scooped separately. Hard on paws for some cats. More expensive upfront but longer lifespan.

Long lasting Low dust Non-clumping

Pine / Wood Pellets

Natural wood or pine fibre pressed into pellets. Absorbs urine and breaks down into sawdust. Natural pine scent. Requires specific sifting litter boxes. Biodegradable. Popular with cat breeders but less common among Malaysian cat owners.

Natural scent Biodegradable Needs sifter box

The Science

Why bentonite wins for Malaysian cat owners

Climate, practicality, and cost all point to bentonite as the superior choice for Malaysia.

Humidity performance

Malaysia's high humidity (80%+) makes litter performance critical. Bentonite's tight clumping prevents odor-causing bacteria from spreading, unlike tofu which can get soggy.

Activated carbon chemistry

Sanking's activated carbon infusion traps odor molecules at the molecular level — not just masking smell with fragrance. This is chemistry, not perfumery.

Best cost per day

When you factor in usage rate, Sanking Carbon Fine Bentonite delivers the lowest cost per day of any premium litter — especially in the 20L format.
