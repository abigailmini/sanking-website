How to Get Rid of Cat Litter Smell at Home: 8 Proven Methods

Updated 6 October 2026 · 9 min read

A litter box should not announce itself. If you can smell your cat's tray from across the room, or guests catch it the moment they walk in, the good news is that it is almost always fixable. Cat litter smell is not inevitable; it is the result of a few specific things going wrong, and once you understand them, eight practical methods will clear it. This guide is written for Malaysian homes, where heat and humidity make odour control harder than in cooler climates.

Why does the litter box smell in the first place?

The sharp, unpleasant smell from a litter box is mostly ammonia. When a cat urinates, bacteria begin breaking down the urea in the urine and release ammonia gas. The longer urine sits exposed, and the warmer and more humid the room, the more ammonia is produced. Faeces add their own odour. So the smell comes from three things: waste sitting too long, a litter that does not seal and absorb odour, and a warm, poorly ventilated space. Every method below attacks one of these.

Method 1: Use an activated carbon clumping litter

This is the single most effective change you can make, so it is first. A litter that clumps fast seals urine before much ammonia is produced, and activated carbon then absorbs the odour molecules that do escape, rather than masking them with perfume. Cheap, slow clumping clay does the opposite: urine pools, ammonia builds, and perfume only layers on top. Switching to a fast clumping carbon bentonite like Sanking often solves most of the smell on its own. Learn more in our odour control guide.

Method 2: Scoop at least twice a day

No litter can mask waste that is left to sit. Scooping morning and night removes clumps and faeces before they have time to off gas. In Malaysia's heat, twice daily is the baseline; once a day is not enough for a fresh home. With a hard clumping litter this takes under a minute each time.

Method 3: Get the litter depth right

Too shallow and urine reaches the tray floor before it can clump, so it pools and smells and sticks to the base. Too deep wastes litter without helping. Aim for about 5 to 7cm of depth so clumps form fully above the tray floor and lift out cleanly.

Method 4: Do a full change and wash on schedule

Even with good scooping, residue builds up over time. Empty the tray completely, wash it with unscented soap and warm water, dry it fully, and refill on a regular schedule, roughly every two to three weeks for one cat and more often for multiple cats. Avoid strong bleach or heavily scented cleaners, which can deter cats and leave their own smell. Our how often to change litter guide has a schedule by cat count.

Method 5: Place the tray in a ventilated spot

Airflow carries odour away before it concentrates. A ventilated bathroom, utility area or balcony nook is far better than a sealed cupboard or a bedroom corner. Never place the tray near where you cook or eat. If the only option is an enclosed space, improve airflow with a small fan or keep a window cracked.

Method 6: Right box, right number

A covered box contains odour and scatter, but must be ventilated or it becomes a smell chamber that the cat then avoids. Follow the one tray per cat plus one rule: two cats need three trays. Too few boxes means each one is overloaded, and overloaded boxes smell and get avoided, which creates worse problems. Multi cat homes should read our multi-cat litter guide.

Method 7: Control the room's humidity

This one is Malaysia specific. High humidity accelerates bacterial activity and ammonia production, and makes some litters, especially tofu, soften and smell faster. Running air conditioning or a dehumidifier in the litter area, or simply improving ventilation, slows odour development noticeably. A drier tray environment also helps clumps stay firm.

Method 8: Skip the cover-up sprays, fix the source

Air fresheners, scented liners and perfumed litters only mask odour temporarily, and many cats dislike strong scents enough to avoid the tray, which makes everything worse. Baking soda sprinkled lightly under the litter can help absorb odour naturally and is cat safe in small amounts, but it is a supplement, not a fix. The real solution is always to remove waste quickly and use a litter that seals and absorbs, not one that perfumes.

Special situations: kittens, senior and multiple cats

Kittens: use a low dust litter and avoid letting them eat large amounts of any litter. Odour is lower with kittens simply because they are small, but build the twice daily scooping habit early. See our kitten litter guide.

Senior cats: older cats may urinate more or have kidney issues that increase odour, and they appreciate a soft fine grain that is gentle on paws. Fast clumping carbon litter keeps up with the extra output.

Multiple cats: odour load multiplies, so this is where method 1 and method 6 matter most. Use a strong carbon bentonite, follow one tray per cat plus one, and consider a larger pack to keep up. A single overloaded tray is the most common cause of a smelly multi cat home.

A note on your cat's health

A sudden change in litter box smell, or urine that smells unusually strong or sweet, can occasionally signal a health issue such as a urinary infection or kidney problem. If odour changes suddenly despite good litter habits, or your cat is urinating more or straining, it is worth a vet visit. Good litter keeps normal odour down; it should not have to mask a medical problem.

Putting it together: a fresh-home routine

  1. Use a fast clumping activated carbon bentonite.
  2. Keep litter 5 to 7cm deep.
  3. Scoop morning and night.
  4. Full change and wash every two to three weeks, sooner for more cats.
  5. Place trays in ventilated spots, one per cat plus one.
  6. Manage humidity with airflow or air conditioning.
  7. Skip masking sprays; a light baking soda layer is optional.

Follow this and the smell disappears, not because you have covered it up, but because you have stopped it being produced. If you live in a condo or apartment where this matters most, our apartment litter guide goes further on small space setups.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I get rid of cat litter smell at home?

The most effective step is using a fast clumping activated carbon litter that seals urine before ammonia forms and absorbs the odour that escapes. Combine it with scooping twice a day, keeping litter 5 to 7cm deep, doing a full change every two to three weeks, placing trays in ventilated spots, and managing humidity. Avoid masking sprays and fix the source instead.

Why does my cat's litter box smell so bad?

The smell is mostly ammonia, produced when bacteria break down urine. It builds up when waste sits too long, when the litter does not clump and absorb odour well, and when the room is warm, humid and poorly ventilated. Malaysia's heat and humidity speed up ammonia production, which is why odour control is harder here.

What litter is best for controlling smell?

A fast clumping bentonite with activated carbon is best, because it seals urine within seconds and the carbon physically traps odour molecules out of the air rather than masking them with perfume. This outperforms crystal, pine, slow clumping clay and perfumed litters, especially in hot, enclosed Malaysian homes.

Does baking soda get rid of litter box smell?

A light layer of baking soda under the litter can help absorb odour naturally and is cat safe in small amounts, but it is a supplement, not a cure. The real fixes are removing waste quickly and using a litter that seals and absorbs odour. Do not overdo baking soda, as some cats dislike it.

How often should I scoop to prevent smell?

Scoop at least twice a day, morning and night, in Malaysia's climate. Once a day is not enough to keep a home fresh because warmth speeds up ammonia production. With a hard clumping litter, each scoop takes under a minute. Do a full tray change and wash every two to three weeks, sooner for multiple cats.

Do air fresheners and scented litter help with litter smell?

Only temporarily, and they can backfire. Air fresheners and perfumed litters mask odour rather than removing it, and many cats dislike strong scents enough to avoid the tray, which creates worse problems. It is far more effective to remove waste quickly and use an unscented activated carbon litter that absorbs odour at the source.
