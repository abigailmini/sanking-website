Where to Buy Cat Litter in Malaysia: Mr DIY vs Speedmart vs Eco Shop vs Online

Updated 6 October 2026 · 9 min read

You have run out of litter and the cat is giving you that look. Where do you actually buy more right now, and where do you get the best value? Malaysian cat owners have more options than ever in 2026, from grabbing a bag at Mr DIY or 99 Speedmart on the way home, to Eco Shop's growing pet aisle, to ordering online from Shopee, Lazada or direct from the brand. Each channel has real trade offs in stock, price and freshness. Here is the honest guide to all of them.

Mr DIY: convenient but limited

Mr DIY has become a surprising go to for cat litter because there is one near almost everyone. The catch is range: you will usually find crystal (silica) litter, some tofu, and basic clumping clay in small to medium bags. It is perfect for an emergency top up when you are out and the shops are closing, but the selection is thin and you rarely find premium bentonite or large value packs. Prices are reasonable on small bags but the cost per kg is not competitive with bulk.

99 Speedmart: for emergencies only

99 Speedmart is everywhere, which is its main virtue. The pet section is small and the litter range is limited, often just a basic clay or crystal option in small bags. If you need litter tonight and nothing else is open, it does the job. For anything beyond an emergency, you will get better value and choice elsewhere.

Eco Shop: cheapest small packs

Eco Shop's fixed low price model now includes a growing pet aisle, and you can sometimes find very cheap small bags of litter. It is the lowest upfront cost option for a single small pack. The trade off is that budget litter is often dusty, slow clumping clay, so while the sticker price is low, the cost per month and the smell and dust in your home can be high. Fine for a backup, not ideal as your main litter.

Shopee and Lazada: choice and delivery

For range, the marketplaces win easily. You can find every type, every brand and every pack size, read thousands of reviews, and have it delivered to your door, which matters when litter is heavy. Watch for a few things: shipping can add up on heavy bentonite, stock can sit in warehouses so freshness varies, and the cheapest listing is not always the real bargain once postage is included. Buying larger packs usually brings the per kg price down and spreads the shipping cost.

Buying direct from the brand: best value and freshness

Buying directly from the manufacturer or its official online store is often the smartest option for your regular litter, as opposed to emergency top ups. You get the full range including large value packs, the freshest stock because it ships from the source rather than sitting in a marketplace warehouse, and the best price per kg on bulk. If you go through a decent volume, bulk direct beats grabbing small bags at convenience stores every week.

Price per kg: the number that actually matters

The biggest mistake Malaysian cat owners make is comparing sticker prices instead of price per kg or per litre. A small RM10 bag feels cheap, but work out the cost per kg and it is often far more expensive than a large pack. Convenience stores and small Eco Shop bags almost always lose this comparison because you pay a premium for the small, grab and go format. Larger packs and bulk, whether from a pet shop, a marketplace or direct from the brand, bring the per kg price down significantly. Before you decide a channel is cheap, divide the price by the weight. The answer often surprises people. We break this down further in our cat litter value for money guide.

How much should you buy at once?

How much to stock depends on your cat count and storage. A single cat household goes through roughly one 10L pack every few weeks, so buying one or two packs at a time keeps stock fresh without overbuying. Multi cat homes burn through litter faster and benefit from larger packs or bulk, since the per kg saving is bigger and you shop less often. The one caveat is humidity: do not stockpile months of tofu, which is humidity sensitive, but refined bentonite stores well sealed in a dry spot. See typical monthly usage in our how much litter per month guide.

Which channel should you use?

  • Out right now, cat waiting: Mr DIY, Eco Shop or 99 Speedmart for a small emergency bag.
  • Want choice and advice: a proper pet shop.
  • Want range plus doorstep delivery: Shopee or Lazada, buying larger packs to offset shipping.
  • Want best value and freshest stock for your regular supply: buy direct from the brand in bulk.

A smart routine many Malaysian owners use: buy their main litter in bulk direct or online, and keep one small emergency bag from Mr DIY as backup so they never run out.

Does where you buy affect quality?

Yes, in two ways. First, freshness. Litter that has sat in a hot warehouse or on a shelf for a long time can perform worse, especially tofu which is humidity sensitive. Buying direct or from high turnover sellers gets you fresher stock. Second, channel limits your options. If you only ever buy at convenience stores, you are stuck with basic crystal and clay and will never experience how much better a premium carbon bentonite performs on odour and clumping. Bottom line

For a one off emergency, Mr DIY, Eco Shop and 99 Speedmart are fine, with Eco Shop cheapest and Mr DIY offering slightly more choice. For range and delivery, Shopee and Lazada are hard to beat. But for your regular litter, buying direct from the brand in bulk gives you the best combination of price per kg, freshness and the full premium range, which is why it is the channel we recommend for anyone past the emergency top up stage.
